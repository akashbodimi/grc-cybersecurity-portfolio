# AI Governance Methodology

> Simulated portfolio methodology for Northstar Cloud Services Pvt. Ltd.

## Objective

Establish a practical governance lifecycle for AI systems covering inventory, context, risk assessment, controls, evaluation, human oversight, third-party dependencies, monitoring and incident response.

## AI RMF Structure

NIST AI RMF 1.0 uses four core functions:

**GOVERN → MAP → MEASURE → MANAGE**

Govern is cross-cutting. The portfolio uses the functions to organize evidence and control ownership rather than treating them as a rigid checklist.

## Governance Lifecycle

**Intake → Inventory → Use-Case Screening → Risk Assessment → Impact Assessment → Control Design → Validation → Approval → Deployment → Monitoring → Reassessment**

## Risk Scoring

For portfolio prioritisation:

**Risk Score = Impact (1–5) × Likelihood (1–5)**

| Score | Tier |
|---:|---|
| 1–5 | Low |
| 6–11 | Medium |
| 12–19 | High |
| 20–25 | Critical |

This is an internal scoring method, not a statutory formula.

## AI-Specific Review Areas

- Purpose and intended use
- Affected people and stakeholders
- Data quality, provenance and minimisation
- Model performance and limitations
- Bias / fairness and potential discriminatory impact
- Explainability / transparency needs
- Human oversight and override
- Security and misuse
- Privacy and confidentiality
- Third-party / GPAI provider risk
- Model and version change management
- Post-deployment monitoring
- Incident and issue handling
- Retention and evidence
- Applicable regulatory requirements

## Regulatory Screening

The portfolio screens EU AI Act applicability by use case rather than assuming that every AI system is treated the same. The legal text has staged application dates and a current consolidated version, so the workbook records screening decisions and directs legal/regulatory questions to qualified reviewers.

## Important Limitation
